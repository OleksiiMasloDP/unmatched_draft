The Genie: Unmatched hero guide

The Genie is an Unmatched hero from Houdini vs. The Genie. Elo rating 1603, ranked #33 of 74 heroes. Playstyle: Value / Tempo / OTK. Complexity: 4/5.

Strengths

Strong base stats for a solo ranged fighter — 16 HP and move 3.

His ability lets him discard a card at the start of his turn for an extra action, entirely on his own terms — unlike similar effects on other fighters that require drawing a specific scheme first, he always chooses when to use it.

His signature scheme is an extremely flexible engine card: it grants a free action, then lets him choose between drawing 5 cards, setting every card's value to 4 for the turn (immune to boosts), or making every opponent discard 2 cards.

Genuine one-turn-kill (OTK) potential — with the right setup he can string together multiple attacks in a single turn backed by a real pool of auto-damage across his deck.

Weaknesses

The ability's cost is real — the discarded card is entirely wasted (not used for damage, trading, or defense), so knowing which cards are safe to sacrifice in a given matchup takes real experience.

The right card to discard, or the right wish to pick, isn't always available in hand when you need it — you have to be ready to adapt rather than follow a fixed plan.

Committing to his max-draw mode effectively shuts off any patient value gameplan for the rest of that turn and commits you to racing the opponent down.

Most heroes have well over 12 HP, so his auto-damage tools alone aren't enough for an OTK — it has to be paired with actually landing real attacks, and one surviving enemy defense card can wreck the whole plan.

General tips

Usually activate your ability before playing Three Wishes — whatever mode you pick, the initiative is about to swing your way, and having the extra action already banked makes it far more effective.

If you're playing a patient value game, lean on the 'opponent discards 2' mode and kite with your move-3 range advantage instead of overcommitting.

Save the full 5-card draw mode for genuine emergencies (you're being crushed and defenses aren't landing) or a real OTK attempt — outside of that it usually isn't worth shutting off your value plan.

Before going for an OTK, actually count what damage you can realistically string together with what's left in your hand and deck — a big draw with no attacks in it accomplishes nothing.

Be careful not to over-draw — as a solo fighter you can't easily corner an opponent, so if your draw rate outpaces how fast you can spend cards, you'll burn through your deck faster than intended.
