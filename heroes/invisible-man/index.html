Invisible Man: Unmatched hero guide

Invisible Man is an Unmatched hero from Cobble & Fog. Elo rating 1192, ranked #67 of 74 heroes. Playstyle: Value / Fatigue / Passive. Complexity: 3/5.

Strengths

Solid map mobility by hopping between his own fog tokens, and gains bonus defense while standing on one.

One of the biggest single auto-damage tools in the game, plus a card that heals him and effectively lets him pass a turn while removing himself from the board.

Doesn't need a great starting position — going first lets him claim fog and a defense bonus early, going second speeds up his passive/attrition plan.

A card that sets an opposing card's value straight to 0, acting as both a strong defense and a solid attack option.

Weaknesses

Poor fine maneuverability — his movement is good for covering distance, but bad for precisely closing in on an opponent.

Weak boost values across his deck.

No scheme/cancel effects at all, which loses him the game outright against a large chunk of the roster.

Very few real attack threats — outside his key auto-damage tools, his best direct attacks are only in the 4-5 range.

His opponent can move his fog tokens too, which can actively work against his positioning.

Prone to awkward hands — sometimes drawing all defense/boost cards with nothing to attack with, or the opposite.

Extremely map-dependent — his ability barely matters on small maps, and he wants a big map to hop across.

General tips

Play a patient, passive game: sit on fog for the defense bonus, use your pass/heal tool to stall and recover HP instead of trading unnecessarily.

Save your big auto-damage and 'set value to 0' cards for when they matter most — they're your main real damage and defensive tools, since your attacks are otherwise weak.

Remember your value-nullifying card only cancels the card's base value, not separate boost or other bonus effects layered on top of it — plan around opponents who have those.

You want a large map to make your fog mobility count; on small maps expect your gameplan to be much weaker.

Because you lack cancel effects entirely, be honest with yourself about matchups where you simply can't interact with the opponent's plan — in those, lean even harder into stalling and racing on health/fatigue rather than trying to fight fairly.
